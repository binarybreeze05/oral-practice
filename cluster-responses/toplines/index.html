The lines in your 1308 cards that most make a rater hear NCLC 7+ (upper B2, a glimpse of C1), best first. Click a line for why and its card.

How they were picked
  • What counts: the NCLC 7 floor rules (replies built on what the examiner or friend just said, audible subjunctives, si + imparfait, concession, precise words, hedges) and what the C1-scored mock-exam candidates say that the B2 ones don't.
  • Every card judged on its own: 37 judges, about 36 cards each. 105 Section A and 206 Section B lines scored 4 or 5 out of 5.
  • Those were re-scored side by side, near-duplicates removed (same move, same structure: the best one stays), then ranked.
  • Why 27 in A and 30 in B: that is where the lists stop adding. The next lines scored lower or repeated a move already on the list twice (a third si + imparfait, a fifth il faudrait que, another ça vaut le coup). B has more because it has more cards (916 to 392) and more kinds of reply.
  • Each French line is copied from its card, unchanged; the English is the card's natural line.
