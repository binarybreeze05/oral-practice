PARAGLIDING

Speaking · Section A · built box 101 words
The prompt, English | French
Prompt

You have read this advertisement for an introduction to paragliding and you are interested. You call to get more information.

PARAGLIDING

On the way to the clouds…

Discover new sensations in paragliding!

On the program of your introduction to paragliding:

- Ground training

- Practical trials

- Series of mini-flights

BOOK AT

450-629-6997

Exceptional rates!

Consigne

Vous avez lu cette publicité pour une initiation au parapente et vous êtes intéressé(e). Vous téléphonez pour avoir plus d’informations.

PARAPENTE

En route pour les nuages…

Découvrez de nouvelles sensations en parapente !

Au programme de votre initiation au parapente :

- Formation au sol

- Essais pratiques

- Séries de mini-vols

RÉSERVEZ AU

450-629-6997

Tarifs exceptionnels !

The box we built (reference)101 words

[examiner: "Hello?"]

Good morning, I'd like to know how much your introduction to paragliding costs.

[examiner: "It depends, we have several packages."]

In that case, which package would you recommend for a complete beginner who's only free on weekends?

[examiner: "For beginners, our one-day introduction, Saturdays or Sundays."]

Noted. Besides, if there were too much wind to take off, what would you do?

[examiner: "We'd postpone the flights to another date."]

So if I understand correctly, it all depends on the wind. Since I live an hour away by car, how far in advance would you warn me?

[examiner: "We'd call you the evening before."]

Okay. And during the mini-flights, will I be alone up there? Even if they're short, I'd like an instructor to be with me, at least the first time.

Your box0 words · built 101
What your box must include

By its last word, your box for the paragliding call must have set the formal register, named the ad, and put every kind of upper B2 evidence on the recording: reactive task moves with one C1 glimpse, a range of marked structures and links, precise topic words, and about 58 seconds of clean speech. All of it has to sit in turns that only make sense after what the advertiser just said, so the rater hears a conversation, not a memorised list.

0 of 25 ticked

The frame

  • Every Section A box uses the same format and the same timing basis, so your point can be measured. Time spent explaining the roles before that line does not count.“The clock starts at the examiner's first line of the section ("Hello?" in A, "Hi! How are you?" in B).”
  • One sentence completes the frame (register set, ad identified) in the fewest words, and the words saved go to evidence later, because the box sits at the time floor. Keep hard grammar out of this turn: raters believe the opener least.“Turn 1 names the ad, sets *vous*, and asks the probe.”
  • Section A is a call to a business, so the frame needs the formal register from the start. Slipping into tu and keeping it up is disaster D8, wrong register.“*Vous* and *votre* from the first sentence, polite conditionals throughout.”

The task moves

  • The examiner's partial answer ("it depends, we have several packages") is what your second turn reacts to, and reacting to a partial answer is the B2 rung. Without the probe there is nothing to react to and the task condition fails.“Without the price question, no partial answer comes.”
  • The rater has to hear that this turn could only follow their reply. Saying their noun with your own quel / quelle avoids lequel, which must agree with a noun you may not have caught; if they name no noun, ask what they would recommend.“The reply to the partial answer must audibly take something from it: a connector ("In that case"), the examiner's own noun ("which package"), or a reword.”
  • A request tied to your situation steers the exchange, which is what lifts a reaction to the B2 rung. Without the anchor the advice question turns generic and could follow any ad.“The request has no anchor in your situation.”
  • Price, length, equipment and location fit any ad, so they carry no "only here" credit. Questions that belong to this activity show you are handling this call, not reciting a general list.“The only-here material has to come from what paragliding actually involves: wind, taking off, landing, flying alone or with an instructor, weight limits.”
  • The C1 glimpse has to be reactive and safe at the same time, and you can only restate a gist you can predict. A less certain gist, such as a reassuring reply, can make a prepared restatement come out wrong.“You can only restate a gist you can predict, and for paragliding one gist is close to certain”
  • This is the C1 glimpse: a well-judged question that combines their answer with your situation. The restatement alone is cheap, and dropping either half leaves the move at plain B2, the coin-flip zone between 6 and 7.“Fix made: the cause clause sits in the same turn as the restatement, so the restatement is used right away.”

Language evidence

  • A marked structure is a grammar form only stronger speakers use reliably, and range across types is what separates the upper B2 box from the lower one. The future and polite conditionals are not counted, so a box built on conditionals alone sounds like correct common complex sentences, which is lower B2.“Syntax: at least four (A) or five (B) different marked structure types, used correctly, at least one inside a reactive turn; at least three kinds of link (P8b).”
  • Evidence in the opener is discounted as rehearsed, while a structure inside a reply turn shows you building it live. The built box carries its restriction (ne... que) in the reply to "it depends" for this reason.“All the hard grammar sits in turns 2 to 5, none in the opener.”
  • Two hard forms in one sentence is where a mid-sentence pause or a wrong form comes from, and that costs delivery. A politeness conditional is a free choice; only the subjunctive depends on another word.“No sentence holds more than one hard one.”
  • The hypothetical turns a stock si clause into evidence tied to this call, and it is the answer your next turn restates. Make it a direct question about what they would do: a "what would happen" question forces the qu'est-ce qui / que choice.“a *si* hypothetical about the one real risk of this activity”
  • A regular -er verb sounds the same in the subjunctive and the indicative in je / tu / il / ils, so it proves nothing on the recording. Shape the English as a wish about what someone else is or does, so it produces que + subjunctive by itself; "to have an X" gives avoir and no subjunctive.“A line counted as carrying a subjunctive must be English whose natural French can only come out audible”
  • Links are their own condition of the point. Plain "and / but / because" do not count, and an "if" clause is syntax, not a link, so it can't be counted twice; a fourth relation gives you margin if one fails.“The links cover four relations: consequence (*dans ce cas*, *donc*), addition (*par ailleurs*), cause (*comme*) and concession (*même si*).”
  • Precise words prove lexis; everyday words (advise, free), words printed on the ad and words the examiner supplies do not count. Use no clichés and no English words: English inside French sentences more than once is disaster D4.“Lexis: about five (A) or six (B) precise words not on the ad, no clichés, no English words; in B, one natural informal expression.”
  • The English is the box, so a precise word counts only if your English produces it on its own. A translator note may choose between natural renderings but never overrule the English; if the English pulls the wrong way, change the English.“Count a precise word only if the English produces it on its own.”

Control and credibility

  • A question that skips over their reply is what a recited list sounds like, and credibility is one of the five conditions of the point. A learned line that ignores the examiner more than once is disaster D7.“Without them your next question ignores their reply, and that is what a recited list sounds like.”
  • Replication shows the evidence is a pattern, not one lucky sentence. Replies weigh most because a turn that only makes sense after their answer cannot have been memorised.“Three moments of clear evidence in three turns, two of them replies (P6).”
  • The rater presumes control only after about 58 seconds of clean speech, so before that the box ends before the point. A turn added only for time adds seconds with nothing in them.“it must earn that time with evidence: a second only-here question, the box's only audible subjunctive and its concession link.”
  • Each piece must hold one of the five conditions or the time floor. Below the floor, cutting does not move the point earlier; the box just ends before it.“So in a box at the floor, a dead word is swapped for evidence (a precise word, a link, an uptake), not deleted; above the floor, dead words go.”
  • Delivery is proved when nothing in the box forces a pause. A word you can't say reliably creates exactly the hesitation the box is there to avoid.“No sentence runs past about 18 French words; no word is rare.”
  • An unhedged invented fact is a target for the examiner. The built box takes "short" from the ad's own "mini", so it claims nothing new.“Anything the ad doesn't print is hedged ("probably", "I'd be surprised if"), asked as a question, or cut.”
  • Your lines must still work whatever they say, or the box sounds recited. Handling an unexpected answer is B2 evidence in itself, and if a reply already answered your next question, swap that question instead of asking what was just answered.“Each examiner reply is followed by a line that still works whatever they say (see the reply table).”
  • The box can only write the most likely version of what they say. A frame lets you say their actual word, so the line never ignores what was said.“Lines to drill as frames, not fixed sentences (standard 13)”

Length

Aim for about 100 English words of your own speech: the built box has 101, about 58 seconds on the shared conversion (English words x 0.58), and its four-turn version of 72 words (about 42 seconds) proved every capacity once but ended before the point, because control was not yet presumed. The point falls at the end of the first turn where all five conditions hold (the built box's fifth turn), about 1:20 to 1:30 after the examiner's "Hello?", counting their short replies and the hand-overs.

After the point

  • Keep asking linked questions for the remaining three minutes or so, each one following up on the last answer.

  • Reserve topics the source lists: how high the mini-flights go and whether they guide you by radio; a weight or age limit; whether the gear (wing, helmet) is provided and what shoes to wear; how many students per instructor; insurance or a medical certificate; where the take-off site is and whether there is parking; how long the exceptional rates last (the ad mentions them); whether booking needs a deposit and whether you would be refunded if you cancelled.

  • If the call runs dry, restate the ad's printed programme order (the flights come after the ground training) and ask whether you would still do the ground training if the wind rose that day.

  • Close only when the examiner signals the end: thank them, say it is clear, and say you will check your dates and call back to book.

  • Sound like a solid NCLC 6 on a quiet day: plain, correct sentences, still asking, still answering, no long silences, no English. Reusing the box's structures, a few gender or agreement slips, quick self-corrections, one request to repeat and one question they had already answered are not disasters.

  • What does pull the score down is a pattern: the examiner restarting you twice in a row or the call ending well before 5 minutes (D5), the same basic error in most turns (D2), long pauses or switching to English (D3, D4), learned lines that ignore what they said (D7), or tu kept up (D8).

Traps

  • Greet with "Good morning" so the French is Bonjour, never Salut.

  • No indirect "what" ("I'd like to know what..."): it invites qu'est-ce que in an indirect question. Use "how much" or another question word. A direct "what would you do" is fine; "what would happen" forces the qu'est-ce qui / que choice.

  • Say the examiner's noun with your own quel / quelle (quel forfait, quelle formule, quelle option), not "which one", which needs lequel to agree with a noun you may not have caught.

  • Ad words never count as precise: parapente, initiation, en route pour les nuages, découvrez de nouvelles sensations, au programme, formation au sol, essais pratiques, séries de mini-vols, réservez au, tarifs exceptionnels. Route is printed, so une heure de route reuses an ad word (à une heure de voiture does not). A word like mini-vols can still anchor a question; it just isn't evidence.

  • "Since" as comme, not puisque: the examiner doesn't already know your reason. "Besides" as par ailleurs or en plus, not aussi, which means "so" at the start of a sentence.

  • A subjunctive from a regular -er verb in je / tu / il / ils is inaudible and proves nothing. Make the English produce que + an audible form; "to have an X" gives avoir and no subjunctive.

  • Watch pronoun reference: a clause placed after "with me" can make its pronoun point to the wrong noun, so put a concession like that at the front of the sentence.

  • Drill the irregular conditionals and the hard sentences until smooth (feriez, préviendriez or vous m'avertiriez, s'il y avait trop de vent pour décoller, the même si... j'aimerais que... soit sentence).

  • Mind gender endings on yourself: débutant / débutante, seul / seule.

  • "Instructor" as moniteur, the usual word at a paragliding school (instructeur is also correct and precise).
  • A translator note only chooses between natural renderings. If your English doesn't produce the French you want, change the English.

B-03

CANOE-CAMPING TRIP

Speaking · Section B · built box 128 words
The prompt, English | French
Prompt

You have read this travel agency advertisement. You are talking about it to a friend with whom you want to go on vacation.

CANOE-CAMPING TRIP

THE TRAVEL AGENCY VACANCES NATURE IS ORGANIZING A WEEK OF « CANOE-CAMPING »

Paddle by day and sleep comfortably in a tent at night.

Experience moments of calm, but also of challenge and adventure! Discover the most beautiful landscapes of the region and fill up on fresh air!

All reservations possible at: www.vacancesnature.ca

Consigne

Vous avez lu cette publicité d’agence de voyage. Vous en parlez à un(e) ami(e) avec qui vous voulez partir en vacances.

SÉJOUR CANOT CAMPING

L'AGENCE DE VOYAGE VACANCES NATURE ORGANISE UNE SEMAINE EN « CANOT-CAMPING »

Pagayez de jour et dormez confortablement dans une tente le soir.

Vivez des instants de calme, mais aussi de dépassement et d'aventure ! Découvrez les plus beaux paysages de la région et faites le plein d'air pur !

Toutes les réservations possibles sur : www.vacancesnature.ca

The box we built (reference)128 words

[examiner: "Hi! How are you?"]

Great! I've found our vacation. It's a week of canoe-camping organized by an agency, Vacances Nature. We paddle during the day, and in the evening we probably pitch the tent on the shore. What I like is that it suits both of us: you want rest; me, I need to move. You're going to tell me that a tent isn't your thing.

[examiner: "Ha! Comfortably, in a tent? I really doubt it."]

I'm not surprised you're skeptical. But with a good inflatable mattress and a warm sleeping bag, you sleep well. Besides, after a whole day in the canoe, you'll fall asleep in two minutes.

[examiner: "A whole day in a canoe? I'm not sporty, I'll be exhausted!"]

Not sporty? That's exactly why it's perfect: unlike a hike, it's the canoe that carries the bags, not your back. And even if you were exhausted, I'd row for both of us.

Your box0 words · built 128
What your box must include

By the last word of your third turn, your second objection reply, the recording must show all five conditions of the point: frame done, every capacity proved, replication, control presumed and credibility intact. For this prompt that means presenting the canoe-camping ad in your own words to a friend you already want to go on vacation with, tied to that shared plan, then defending it against two objections you have steered yourself, with prepared replies that still answer what the friend just said.

0 of 25 ticked

The frame

  • Ignoring the greeting makes your first line sound launched from a script, which costs credibility (condition 5). One word shows you heard them, and anything more only delays the ad.“Greeting in B: one word”
  • The prompt sets up a friend you already want to travel with, and "present the document to a friend" means presenting it to this person. Without the tie, the presentation is no longer aimed at the friend and the frame (condition 1) is weaker.“ties the ad to an open plan the two of you share”
  • The frame is only done when the ad is identified and presented in your own words, not read. Reshaping the printed sentence is what makes it rewording, and the passive participle also gives you one of your five structure types.“The ad is not identified: who organizes it, what, how long (C1).”
  • This is a conversation with a friend, and vous kept up with a friend is a disaster on its own (D8). Setting tu early is also why a filler opener such as "Listen" could be cut.“Register: tu, on, informal markers.”

The task moves

  • Section B asks for a detailed presentation, and the ad is only four lines, so the detail has to come from this order and not from new facts. A one-sentence presentation is the B1 "simple presentation", not the detailed one.“a presentation in your own words with a clear order (what it is, how a day goes, why it suits the two of you, what you expect to hear back)”
  • The presentation has to say why the trip fits the two of you (P17b), and a bare claim that it suits you both is the B1 "arguments clear but little developed". Matching the ad's own promises to each of you holds up even if the friend denies the portrait, because the ad's promise still stands.“P17b wants the presentation to say why it fits the two of you”
  • The examiner is told to leave you the initiative and to disagree, so they will almost always take a ready-made objection that is surely on their list, and your first reply becomes one you can prepare. Naming two at once forces a speech-length reply and leaves the second objection to chance, and a question after the hand-over invites something you did not plan (standard 3).“The presentation ends by naming the objection the ad invites (the tent).”
  • Opening on their words makes the turn audibly a reply and not a recited line, which protects credibility (condition 5). Without it, a prepared reply sounds like a new prepared argument and the rater starts to hear a script (D7).“Every objection reply opens on the friend's words (their noun, a reword, 'even if you...'), so it is audibly a reply and not a recited line.”
  • A reply with no concrete counter leaves the objection unanswered, so the task criterion's "illustrated" fails. Making the counter something you bring keeps it from turning into an invented fact about the trip.“Reply 1 has no concrete counter, so the comfort objection is unanswered (C2 task: illustrated).”
  • This line makes the second objection predictable: without it you need about four prepared second replies instead of one, and the addition link goes. It is the cheapest piece to cut, and cutting it still breaks a condition.“Without it the second objection is no longer called (four prepared second replies instead of one) and the addition link goes.”
  • Most arguments for this ad fit any outdoor trip, so the task needs one only this trip allows. Spent in the presentation, it is gone when the objection it answers best arrives, and you end up repeating yourself, the booklet's named weakness.“Most arguments for this ad (nature, disconnecting, adventure, a change of scene) fit any outdoor trip.”
  • The B2 column names defending clearly and with determination, and a commitment shows it where a joke would not. Putting the friend's own word inside the hypothetical means the line cannot be recited without having heard them.“Together they are the 'defends clearly and with determination' the B2 column names.”
  • Arguments of different kinds are the one C1 feature the task criterion needs, and they come from replies you need anyway. One glimpse is enough (standard 16), so words spent on a second one only push the point later.“Arguments of four kinds (it suits both of us, gear, a comparison only a canoe allows, a commitment) give the one C1 feature this criterion needs, without extra words.”

Language evidence

  • Five types is the Section B minimum for the syntax criterion, so in a box at five none can go. Two sentences from the same family prove one type, not two.“at least four (A) or five (B) different marked structure types, used correctly, at least one inside a reactive turn”
  • At least one structure has to sit inside a reactive turn, and with one in each reply the syntax count does not depend on which objection comes. A reply is also the least predictable place in the box, so a structure there is the hardest to dismiss as recited.“Every fallback keeps one hard structure in each reply (an audible subjunctive in reply 1, *même si* + imperfect + conditional in reply 2)”
  • A subjunctive that sounds like the indicative proves nothing on the recording. The built box dropped a line about getting that the friend wasn't convinced, which can come out as a plain indicative, for one whose verb forces sois.“A line counted as carrying a subjunctive must be English whose natural French can only come out audible”
  • Under the strict count only connectors that join clauses or turns count: plain and, but and because don't, and neither does a preposition before a noun such as contrairement à. Section B needs three relations with a concession among them, and the box found two of its three by swapping words it already had, at no cost.“In Section B one of the three must be a concession.”
  • Every content word on the ad is topic vocabulary (pagayer, tente, dormir, calme, aventure, paysages, air pur, canot), so the obvious words earn nothing. The count has to stay honest to give a real margin: the friend's word for tired and everyday words such as se reposer, bouger, s'endormir, sacs, dos don't count.“Your precise words have to come from what the ad leaves out.”
  • The Section B lexis condition asks for one natural informal expression, and it widens your range beyond lower B2's "everyday repertoire". Dropping ne is normal with a friend.“in B, one natural informal expression.”
  • The English is the box, so a line that needs a note to overrule it will come out wrong under pressure. The built box changed "paddle" to "row" so it gives je ramerais, and put the "me" up front in English so the stressed pronoun appears without a note.“The English is the box, so every line must produce the intended French on its own.”
  • Delivery is proved when nothing in the box forces a pause. A long sentence or a hard verb form (je pagaierais) is exactly where a pause or a false start would come.“Delivery: no sentence over 18 English words, each breaks at its commas, and every verb form is easy (je ramerais, not je pagaierais).”

Control and credibility

  • The examiner knows the ad, so an unhedged invented fact (price, a guide, the region, showers, phone network) is a target. A hypothetical about what you would do is safe because it makes no claim about the trip.“Inventing any of these is a risk (P18, standard 9): the examiner knows the ad.”
  • Replication (condition 3) needs three moments of evidence in three turns, two of them replies; with only one reply it fails and "defends firmly" is unproved. Linked turns are also how control (condition 4) is presumed, since it can only be disproved later.“Replication: three turns carry evidence, two of them replies to the friend.”
  • The box assumes the examiner takes the weak spot you named, and when they don't, the same frames keep one hard structure in each reply so the syntax count holds. A learned line that ignores what they just said, more than once, is the script showing (D7).“That the examiner takes the tent (if not, the fallbacks in Working keep the same frames: a grant with an audible subjunctive, then 'even if' + imperfect + conditional).”
  • A dead word only pushes the point later. The built box reaches its point against an examiner who behaves as the booklet says because nothing in it is spare.“If an element can be removed and all five still hold, it was a dead word and goes.”

Length

Aim for about 128 English words, the built box's count (roughly 63 in the presentation and about 32 in each reply), which is about 74 seconds of your speech at English words x 0.58; below about 100 to 104 words the box simply ends before the point instead of moving it earlier, because control needs about 60 seconds of your speech. The point falls at the end of your third turn, your second objection reply, about 1:30 after the friend's "Hi! How are you?", leaving about eight and a half minutes after it.

After the point

  • Keep answering in plain, correct French for the rest of the section, still answering what the friend says; reusing the box's structures and quick self-repairs are fine.
  • Keep the initiative and close the loop: suggest looking at the agency's website together and booking.
  • Bring fresh arguments, each one new: switching off (a whole week, not a weekend), the landscapes in your own words, the price (hedged: it isn't on the ad, compare it with a week in a hotel), places a canoe reaches that have no road (hedged), something in the trip for each of you.
  • If the friend quotes the ad's "comfortably" again, use irony on that word.
  • Use your prepared fallbacks for effort first, safety, insects, rain, time and price.
  • Never use the same argument three times; repeating one once is fine.
  • If pushed hard late on, a compromise is fine (asking the agency about beginners), but never give in and drop the plan.
  • You don't have to convince the friend: the grid rewards defending, not winning.

Traps

  • After the feeling verb, say the subjunctive (sois), never que tu es; repair at once if it slips.
  • Même si takes the imperfect, never the conditional (même si tu serais); if it slips, repair with pardon and go on.
  • Pick verbs that are easy in the conditional: ramer (je ramerais), not pagayer (je pagaierais).
  • Repeat the friend's adjective in the form they used (sportif or sportive); for tired, take their word (épuisé and crevé sound the same in either gender).
  • Say canot, the ad's word, in canot-camping.
  • No indirect "what": "I know what you'll say" invites qu'est-ce que in an indirect question; name the weak spot as something the friend will tell you. A pseudo-cleft for what you like (ce qui me plaît) is fine.
  • No question after naming the weak spot.
  • Avoid calques: "That's exactly the point" comes out as a calque; English that gives c'est justement pour ça que gives you a consequence link instead.
  • In the safety fallback, use a verb whose subjunctive is audible (puisse), not one that sounds like the indicative (laisse); in the rain fallback, avoid au cas où + conditional.
  • If asked the price, what is included or whether you pitch the tent yourselves, say what the ad doesn't say and hedge; add no new fact.
  • No English words inside French sentences.

EA-019

Discoveries & secrets

Writing · Section A · built box 66 words
The prompt, English | French
Prompt

Last Saturday, Mehdi, a man in his seventies, was walking his dog near the lake when the dog made a very strange discovery…

Consigne

Samedi dernier, Mehdi, septuagénaire, promenait son chien près du lac quand celui-ci a fait une découverte bien étrange…

The box we built (reference)66 words

By digging in the mud, the animal unearthed a rusty chest. The retiree couldn't believe his eyes, for he had hidden it with his class sixty years earlier.

“Even though we had planned to open it in 2000, nobody remembered the spot,” the former pupil explained. Mehdi has brought it to the town hall to find his schoolmates, several of whom reportedly live in the region.

Your box0 words · built 66
What your box must include

By its last word, your box has delivered the "very strange discovery" the printed start promises: what Mehdi's dog found, why that is strange, why it stayed lost, and what happens now. It does this as a news report, with a quote and a journalistic conditional, so every NCLC 7 condition for Section A is met before your plain rest begins.

0 of 24 ticked

The frame

  • Continuing straight on is a Section A gate, and contradicting the printed start is a missing-task disaster. Opening with a quote instead pushes the discovery out of the first sentence, which should carry the answer.“The first sentence continues straight on from the printed '…' with new information, nothing restated.”
  • The news voice is part of the Section A gate. Narrating as je outside a quote is a register or genre break that pulls the score down even after the point.“in A, narrating as *je* outside quotes”
  • The task asks for several paragraphs, and the box has to close that gate on its own. In the built box the break comes after the second sentence, once the find and its twist are told.“the paragraph break sits inside the box”

The task moves

  • The Section A rule says the box must contain whatever the printed start sets up as the story's content, and here that is the discovery. A plain closing line can wait for the rest, because writing it needs no skill.“If the start promises a discovery, the discovery”
  • Tying the twist to his age makes the content belong to this prompt, so it cannot read as a template. The obvious answer (old banknotes, the police, a reward) is the one graders have read a hundred times, and it is not strange.“That twist uses the only fact the prompt gives about Mehdi (his age), so the content is tied to this prompt and cannot read as a template.”
  • That is the NCLC 7 narration line, "a detailed account marking how events relate": a 6 tells events in order, a 7 marks why each one follows. When the story carries the links, you need no added sentences to supply them.“Each sentence explains the one before it: found, why astonishing, why it stayed lost, what happens now.”
  • Coverage needs one developed idea (P13), and in the built box the twist's reason and consequence are that idea. They also answer a sceptical grader's "precise, not detailed": the strangeness the start promises is explained, not just stated.“Developed: the twist with its reason (planned for 2000, the spot forgotten) and its consequence (the search).”
  • Coverage needs a precise detail, and each of these does its own job: without the property the find lands as a bare noun, and without the time span the hiding could be last week and the strangeness goes. A second detail giving the same precision ("iron" next to "rusty") carries nothing and goes.“Details that are the only carrier of a number, an age or a place stay.”
  • The quote is one of the two news conventions; the floor asks for one and ideally both. In the tag, a-t-il expliqué is an inversion trap and his name a second time would be a repeat; in the built box the quote also carries the second plus-que-parfait and the concession, so dropping it costs all three.“a genre mood (the journalistic conditional) or a quote with the speech tag after it, ideally both”
  • It is the second news convention, the French habit of giving an unconfirmed fact in the conditional, and it is also the nuance coverage asks for.
